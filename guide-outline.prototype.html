Three ways to cut the guide

Same seven jobs in every scheme. What changes is how they are grouped, how the reader is expected to move through them, and what the table of contents has to carry. Switch with the bar at the bottom.

A — Flat seven

Seven peer sections, one scroll, one level of table of contents. Every section is a deep-link target and no section contains another. The reader is assumed to be arriving at a section, not reading from the top.

TABLE OF CONTENTS — 7 entries, one level, fits the existing sticky subheader
Read a marked sleeve Tell Pool from Core Choose your slots and corner Run a marking session Count the copies you need Mark a split group Fix a mark you no longer want

B — Three acts

The same seven jobs, grouped under three headings that name the reader's state: understanding the system, marking cards, keeping it current. Deep links still target the seven leaves. The acts give a first-time reader a shape and let a returning reader skip a whole act.

TABLE OF CONTENTS — 3 groups, 7 leaves, two levels
Costs a layout change. A two-level list cannot live in the horizontally scrolling slot="subheader" the step bar uses today, and slot="navigation" is taken by the site nav that layout.js injects. Scheme B needs a table of contents block at the top of <main> instead of the sticky bar.

C — Procedure first, reference below

One short numbered procedure at the top covering the whole physical job, each step one line and each step a link down. Everything else sits below it as un-numbered reference sections in any order. The page serves two readers at once: one follows it, one consults it.

TOP OF PAGE — the procedure block, strict mode, one line per step
  1. Finalize every decklist. Do not edit a deck during a marking session.
  2. Choose the corner your marks start from. This choice is permanent.
  3. Give your most-played decks the slots you can read fastest.
  4. Test your pen on a spare Perfect Fit inner.
  5. Mark each copy, working down the results list.
  6. Let the paint dry, then double-sleeve.
Each step links to the reference section that explains it. Reference sections are the same seven from A.
The risk to weigh. A numbered block at the top is close in shape to the 1-to-7 walkthrough the map already ruled out. The defence is that these steps are physical rather than app instruction, so nothing in the block goes stale when the interface changes. Decide whether that distinction survives contact with a reader.
